# Phase 22 Playground (the phase gate scene)

Generated by `inf_editor_core::samples::phase22_playground_scene` -- the
**composed** gate scene for Phase 22 (dynamic world: deformation & destruction),
and the plan's own done-when sentence built: *a playground scene shows footprints
and tyre tracks in snow and sand, bending grass, and a car and a multi-storey
building destroyed by Blueprint-triggered explosions with debris physics --
deterministic on the replay trace, PIE == shipping.*

## What is in it

- `Phase22Playground.inf_lvl` -- a 128 m square of **asset-backed (streamed)**
terrain, flat where anything stands, painted in two bands: **snow** (layer 3,
the deep non-recovering archetype) over `z` 40-56 and **sand** (layer 2, the
loose one that slumps over minutes) over `z` 56-64, grass everywhere else --
so the roller's straight run crosses both and ends back on ground that barely
dents, which is what makes 'the trail has a far end' a testable claim.
- `Phase22Playground.inf_terrain` -- that heightfield and its splat weights. The
terrain is asset-backed because this is the **streamed** configuration, and
because the P22.3 physics bridge turns sim-resident level-0 tiles into
heightfield colliders: everything in this level that falls, lands on those.
- `Block.inf_mesh` -- a 6 x 9 x 6 m tessellated box, three storeys of three
metres, 3 072 triangles. **Authored geometry, not the P19 grammar**: a
`Destructible` fractures the ONE mesh its actor's `MeshRef.asset` names, and the
grammar produces a scattered *population* with no merged mesh anywhere in the
pipeline -- so using it would have meant building a grammar-to-mesh bake first,
which is a modelling feature and belongs to Phase 23.
- `Chassis.inf_mesh` -- the car's 4 x 1 x 2 m body, likewise tessellated (both
meshes clear the cook's 2 048-triangle vgeom threshold, because the gate's
cook-silence arm would otherwise fail on a sub-threshold advisory).
- `Demolition.inf_act` -- the charge. On Tick, over steps 60-65, it spends
80 kJ a tick on its own actor's bonds and then reports `is_intact` and
`chunk_count`; its `Destroyed` handler counts its firings and plays the actor's
`AudioSource`. **Two actors carry this one class**: the block, and a control
twin 24 m away whose `Destructible::runtime_destruct` is `false`. They differ in
that flag and in nothing else -- not even in what they are made of -- which is
what makes the control a control.
- `Wreck.inf_act` -- the car bomb: ONE instant at step 60, 30 kJ of damage plus
an 8 kN.s radial impulse at the car's own place with a 12 m radius. Both
'once' and the size are the physics: an explosion is one impulse, and firing
it on every tick of a six-tick window multiplied its momentum by six -- which
the gate measured as a wheel 60 m up. The blast reaches the chassis' debris
and its four wheels and stops well short of the block, so 'the block's chunks
moved' stays a statement about the collapse.
30 kJ is one chunk's worth of bonds, and what happens next is the point: a car
body is not supported by static geometry, so the eleven chunks still attached
are hanging on four DYNAMIC wheels the support rule refuses to count, and the
structural solve drops the lot.
- `Roller.inf_act` -- the thing that leaves the tracks. Every Tick it writes its
own velocity (`physics3d.set_velocity`): 8 m/s along +Z and a steady 1 m/s
down, so it is *pressed into* the surface rather than balanced on it. Its body
has `gravity_scale = 0`, which makes the run a scripted probe: the only
vertical input is the one the script writes.
- `Collapse.inf_audio` -- the short deterministic tone the `Destroyed` handler
plays (the same generator the physics playground commits, so no binary fixture
is needed).
- A **car**: a destructible chassis with four wheels on revolute joints. It is a
**prop, not a vehicle** -- this engine has no vehicle controller -- so it
settles onto its wheels and is then blown up, which is all the phase claims.
Its colliders carry **authored densities** (rubber 1100 for the wheels, 150 for
the hollow chassis shell). If you copy one thing out of this sample, copy that:
`Collider3D::density` defaults to **1.0 kg/m3**, which is rapier's mass
placeholder and lighter than air. A 0.4 m wheel at the default weighs 268
grams, and an impulse sized against 5-tonne fracture chunks throws it out of
the level. (A chunk's mass never comes from there -- it comes from
`Destructible::density_kg_m3`, which exists for exactly this reason.)
- A **grass strip** straddling the roller's lane, so instances stand both inside
the trail and clear of it: the P22.1 bend shader reads the same deformation
window the terrain displacement does, and a strip entirely inside the rut would
bend uniformly and prove nothing.

## What it is NOT

No dust and no smoke: this engine has no particle system, so a break makes a
sound and no visual effect. That is ledgered rather than faked. The sub-chunk
**rubble** P22.4 adds is real, but it is instanced render dressing derived from
the detach events -- it is not simulated and never feeds back into the sim.

Destruction is **not persisted**: rubble dies with the session, and saving after
a break produces a byte-identical `.inf_lvl`
(`inf-editor-core`'s `simulate_destruction_not_persisted`).

## The gate (`runtime/inf-player/tests/phase22_gate.rs`)

Two loads bit-identical; cooked == uncooked **up to the charge** (a dev directory
has no `.inf_fracture` by design) and demonstrably different past it; PIE ==
shipping on the full trace (deformation field bytes + every destructible's
`FractureState` bits + the fixed step's audit counters + ground probes) compared
as raw bits, per step; a REAL `--pie` subprocess against the in-process
reference; the world assertions (the charge OPENED the block and left a
structure behind, the car fractured and the structural solve dropped what the
charge did not pay for, the trail is where the roller ran and nowhere else, the
debris budget held, the control twin survived the identical charge); budgets;
pool-size determinism; and the save-after-damage identity.

Regenerate with `INF_BLESS_SAMPLES=1 cargo test -p inf-editor-core samples`.
